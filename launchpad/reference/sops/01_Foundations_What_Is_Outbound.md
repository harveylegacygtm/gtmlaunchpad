## The three acquisition channels

Every B2B business gets customers through some combination of three channels:

**Outbound - you bring the conversation to them.** You decide who to talk to, you reach out first, you control the volume and the timing. Cold email is outbound. Cold calling is outbound. LinkedIn outreach is outbound. Instagram DM is outbound. Direct mail is outbound.

**Inbound - they come to you.** You build something - content, SEO presence, social brand, community - that attracts the right people, and they reach out when they're ready. Blog posts, podcasts, YouTube, organic social, SEO, free tools that bring traffic. All inbound.

**Paid - you rent attention.** You pay platforms (Google, Meta, LinkedIn, podcasts, newsletters, sponsorships) to put your message in front of audiences that match your ICP. They click, they fill out a form, they enter your funnel. All paid.

Most successful businesses run all three. But they sequence them, weight them, and run them differently. Knowing the difference matters because the rules of each channel are completely different.

## What makes outbound different

Three things make outbound fundamentally different from inbound and paid. These differences explain why outbound works the way it works, and why the SCOPE method is built the way it is.

### **1. Direct response**

Outbound is direct response. You know who you're talking to (their name, their company, their role). You know when you're talking to them (you control send time). You can measure exactly what happens (did they reply? did they book? did they close?). Every email is a unit. Every reply is data. Every campaign is a test.

Inbound and paid are different. With inbound, you're trying to influence behavior at scale through content that may or may not reach the right person, and you can rarely attribute revenue cleanly. With paid, you can attribute, but the unit economics get fuzzy fast - by the time you account for ad fatigue, frequency capping, and creative refresh cycles, the math gets harder to forecast.

Outbound math is simple: send X emails, get Y replies, get Z calls, close N deals. The funnel is the funnel. That's the property that lets us treat it as a system instead of a campaign.

### **2. Automatable**

Outbound is the only one of the three where you can fully automate the operational layer. Finding leads, enriching them, sending sequences, handling replies - all of it has been productized into tools. You can run a large outbound system with a small team because the leverage points are software-driven.

Inbound takes labor. Real humans writing real content, recording real videos, building real audiences. There's no shortcut. AI helps, but the floor is still high - your audience smells AI slop and bounces.

Paid takes capital and creative. Real budget out the door, real production cycles, real platform expertise to navigate every algorithm change. You can scale paid fast, but you can't scale it cheap.

Outbound is the only channel where the marginal cost of one more email approaches zero, and the marginal cost of one more lead is a function of data costs that drop with volume. That's why it's the leverage move for early-stage and scaling B2B teams - and a channel enterprise B2B can dominate with strong economies of scale and branding.

### **3. You control the volume dial**

With outbound, you decide how much pipeline you want this week. Want more? Send more. Want less? Send less. The dial is in your hand.

With inbound, you don't control timing. You publish a post and hope it ranks in six months. You release a video and hope it lands. You build an audience over months and years.

With paid, you control spend, but the platforms control delivery. Algorithms shift. Costs rise. Your CAC last quarter has nothing to do with your CAC this quarter.

Outbound respects your timeline. That makes it the right first channel for any B2B business that has product-market fit, a working sales process, and needs predictable pipeline now.

## **Where AI fits in modern outbound**

Five years ago, outbound at scale required a small army: lead researchers, list builders, copywriters, email senders, reply handlers, sales reps. Today, AI does most of that work, and the small army becomes one or two operators with a stack.

# Specifically, AI now handles:

- **Account research and list building.** Pulling target companies, enriching them with technographic and behavioral signals, scoring them against your ICP, surfacing buying triggers. What used to take a researcher 30 minutes per company now takes 30 seconds.

- **Lead qualification.** Pass/fail filtering against ICP criteria. Right person at a fitting company? Right buying signals? Reject the bad ones automatically.

- **Lead scoring.** 1–10 ranking on fit. Used to require a sales-ops team building rubrics in Salesforce. Now done in a Clay column with an AI prompt.

- **Personalized messaging at scale.** AI reads each company's website, recent news, and leadership posts, then writes a custom hook per lead. Not generic AI compliments ("I love your mission") - specific, factual observations relevant to the prospect's situation.

- **Sales enablement before the call.** Pull every closed-won case study, match the most relevant one to the prospect's industry and size, drop it into the rep's prep doc before the discovery call.

- **Post-call analysis.** Transcribe every sales call, surface common objections, extract the prospect's exact language for next month's copy refresh. The sentiment loop that closes outbound's feedback cycle.

- **Creative touches at the edges.** AI-generated handwritten letters printed by mail services. Personalized video intros. Voicemails in a cloned voice. The layer that separates a good campaign from a memorable one.

What AI hasn't replaced - and won't replace soon - is the strategic layer. Picking the right ICP. Constructing the right offer. Writing the right messaging principles. Reading what's working in the market right now and adjusting the system. That's where the operator's judgment still earns its keep, and that's what The Launchpad teaches you.

## Why we're betting on outbound (the OIP system, briefly)

Our methodology says: do outbound first. Then layer in inbound. Then layer in paid.

There's a full SOP on this - see **SOP 02**. The short version: outbound gives you predictable pipeline and direct customer feedback that you then use to build inbound and paid that actually convert. Starting with inbound or paid means guessing at messaging, ICP, and offer. Outbound forces you to learn those at the only speed they can be learned: real conversations with real prospects.

Outbound first is not "outbound only." It's "outbound is the leading edge of your acquisition system." Once it's running, you bolt on the others.

## What outbound is not

A few things to disambiguate, because terminology gets fuzzy:

**Outbound is not ****"****spam.****"** Done right, outbound is targeted, relevant, and respectful. You're emailing the right person, about a real problem they have, with a useful proposal. Spam is the opposite - untargeted, irrelevant, volume-only. 

The difference matters because the systems and laws (CAN-SPAM, GDPR, etc.) treat them differently. We always operate inside the lines.

**Outbound is not ****"****appointment setting****"**** alone.** Some agencies use these terms interchangeably. Appointment setting is one outcome of outbound (book a call). Outbound is the broader system: list building → messaging → sending → reply handling → call → close. Appointment setting is one stage; outbound is the whole.

**Outbound is not just cold email.** Cold email is the dominant channel because it's the most automatable and scalable. But outbound includes cold calling (which we run alongside email for many clients), LinkedIn outreach, direct mail, and any other proactive channel. 

The Launchpad is email-first because email is where the leverage is, but the methodology applies to other channels with adjustments.

**Outbound is not magic.** It's math. It's the funnel. It's volume × conversion × time. Every "trick" on LinkedIn that promises 50% reply rates with one weird subject line is selling you a magic story. The truth is less sexy: targeted list, relevant offer, clear copy, proper infrastructure, enough volume, good reply handling. That's the whole game.
