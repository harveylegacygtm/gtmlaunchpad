## Why two offers, not one

When most people say "the offer," they mean the thing they're selling - the SaaS, the service, the package. That's your core offer. But in cold outbound, the core offer isn't what you pitch first. You pitch the next step - usually a call. The thing that makes that call worth booking is the lead magnet: what they get on or before the call that's valuable enough to give up 30 minutes.

**Two different problems.** The core offer must convert calls to deals - priced, scoped, and positioned right for your ICP. The lead magnet must get a cold prospect interested enough to book - specific, valuable, easy to deliver. Most people obsess over the core offer and never think about the lead magnet, then wonder why their reply-to-meeting ratio is garbage.

## The order of operations

You don't optimize all your offers at once. Build them in sequence:

- **Core offer first.** This generates revenue. If it isn't right, nothing downstream matters.

- **Lead magnet second.** Once the core offer converts calls, design a magnet that books more calls. The magnet exists to feed the core offer.

- **Retention play third.** Renewals, upsells, expansion. Long-term, this is where most B2B revenue actually lives.

- **Low-ticket tripwire last.** A $50–500 offer that lets cold prospects buy small before buying big. A scaling tool, not a starting one.

The mistake most founders make: starting with low-ticket to "scale fast." Until you have a core offer that reliably converts calls to deals, low-ticket eats all your time and produces little revenue. Most businesses may never need a low-ticket offer.

## The core offer - the gate

Before you make an offer irresistible, it has to clear three pass/fail tests. These don't make it good - they confirm you have one.

- **Is it a real solution to a real pain?** Refer back to the pain research in (SOP 06). If your offer doesn't solve one of the pains you identified, you have a product looking for a problem.

- **Is it priced right for the value delivered?** Cold prospects won't do the math for you. The classic frame: 10x the value, 1x the price. If it delivers $100K of value, price it $5K–$25K, not $80K. The clearer the ROI, the easier the sale.

- **Is it specific enough to picture?** "Marketing consulting" means nothing. "We rebuild your cold outbound to deliver 30+ booked calls a month within 90 days" is a thing the prospect can picture and compare. Specificity sells.

Fail any of these and fix the offer before anything else. No clever copy compensates for a fuzzy or mispriced offer. Once it passes the gate, you make it irresistible with the next tool.

## The Value Equation - how to make any offer irresistible

The three tests get you to "sellable." They don't get you to "irresistible." For that there's one tool worth more than every other offer trick combined: the Value Equation. 

We didn't invent it - that's Alex Hormozi's - but it's the cleanest way to think about why some offers fly and some die, so we use it as the spine of this module.

**Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort ****&**** Sacrifice)**

Four dials. Two up, two down. Most weak offers aren't badly priced - they're just leaving three of the four untouched.

- **Dream Outcome (turn UP).** The biggest, most specific result they want. Not "more leads" - "30 booked sales calls with your exact ICP, every month." Tie it to their number. Vague outcomes are worth nothing because they can't be pictured.

- **Perceived Likelihood (turn UP).** It doesn't matter how good the outcome is if they don't believe it'll happen for them. Raise it with proof (case studies in their exact niche), specificity, and - the heavy hitter - risk reversal.

- **Time Delay (turn DOWN).** How long until the first win. "Results in 6–12 months" is a hard offer. "First booked call within 14 days, full pipeline by day 90" is alive. If results take time, find an early win and lead with it.

- **Effort ****&**** Sacrifice (turn DOWN).** Every step you make them do lowers the value. This is why done-for-you beats done-with-you beats do-it-yourself - and why you can charge 3x for the same outcome by removing the labor.

The fastest wins are almost always on dials 2, 3, and 4. Founders obsess over the outcome and forget that belief, speed, and ease are where the offer actually becomes irresistible.

## Reverse the risk - the highest-leverage dial

The single fastest way to spike perceived likelihood is to take the risk off the prospect and put it on yourself. In cold outbound, the prospect's #1 silent objection is always "this won't work for my business." Four flavors, roughly in order of aggression:

**The Guarantee Ladder.** Four ways to make "yes" feel safe. The higher you climb, the more you de-risk the buyer, and the more it costs you if you can't deliver.

- **4 - Performance / pay-on-results.** "You only pay per qualified call booked." Removes risk almost entirely - only offer it once your system reliably delivers.

- **3 - Unconditional guarantee.** "Don't love it in 30 days? Full refund." Rare in services, nuclear when you can afford it.

- **2 - Conditional guarantee.** "If we don't book you X calls in 90 days, we work for free until we do." Strong default for services, ties your pay to their result.

- **1 - Implied guarantee.** The custom lead magnet itself (free audit, free pilot) is a soft guarantee - they see the goods before they pay a cent. Always available, your lead magnet doing double duty.

Rule: never offer a guarantee you can't survive paying out at scale. The point isn't to give money back; it's to make saying yes feel safe enough that the prospect stops stalling. The more saturated your market, the more work the guarantee does.

## The lead magnet - the bait that gets the call

The lead magnet is what makes a stranger willing to give you 30 minutes. One design rule matters above all:

**A lead magnet is a complete solution to one narrow problem - that reveals the next one.** It has to be genuinely valuable (so good they'd have paid for it), narrow (it fully solves one specific problem), and it has to expose the bigger problem only your core offer solves. The best magnets leave the prospect thinking "if the free thing is this good, I need the paid thing."

A magnet does one of three jobs (pick one):

- **Reveal a problem (diagnose).** Show them something broken they didn't know about → the free audit. Most powerful in cold outbound, because the diagnosis is the qualification.

- **Give a sample (a taste).** Hand over a small, real piece of the result → free creative, free strategy, free pilot.

- **Do one step of a multi-step process.** Complete step 1 for them; the core offer is steps 2 through 10 → "We build your target list; the campaign that works it is what we run for you."

Because the magnet is itself a mini-offer, run it through the Value Equation too. The best magnets are high dream outcome, high likelihood (specific to the prospect), near-zero time delay (delivered live on the call), and near-zero effort. That's exactly why custom-on-a-call beats a 50-page PDF they have to read.

### Custom lead magnets (recommended in 2026)

A custom magnet is built for the prospect using their specific company, situation, or data:

- **Free audit.** "We'll audit your current cold email setup and tell you the 3 biggest issues, on a 30-min call."

- **Free creative.** "We'll write 3 cold email variants for your business and you keep them whether or not we work together."

- **Free strategy / analysis.** "We'll build a 90-day GTM plan (or pull a list of 1,000 scored prospects) and walk you through it on the call."

Why custom wins now: AI flooded the market with generic PDFs and ebooks. Prospects stopped opening them. A custom artifact built for their company cuts through because it's clearly not mass-produced - and on the Value Equation it's higher on every dial. The unlock: **AI now lets you mass-produce a bespoke magnet per prospect** - the audit, the creative, the scored list - so "custom" finally scales.

**The disguise principle.** When you deliver a free strategy or pilot on a call, the prospect comes for the strategy. 

While you deliver it, you're also:

(a) qualifying them

(b) demonstrating expertise

(c) finding their specific pain

(d) surfacing how your paid offer fits. 

They feel served. You feel sold. The trick: deliver the magnet ON the call, not by email. Email it and they usually read it and don't book.

### Automated lead magnets (still useful, lower leverage)

The traditional category - PDFs, ebooks, courses, templates, calculators. Still works for top-of-funnel awareness, at-scale economics (no human time per delivery), retargeting/nurture capture, and less competitive markets. 

It fails in crowded spaces where every competitor has the same PDF, in high-ticket sales where buyers want attention, and in markets where AI-generated content is distrusted. 

In 2026, default to custom unless you have a strong reason to go automated.

## Offer and lead magnet by campaign type

Here's the bridge to the next doc. The offer/magnet choice maps directly onto the two campaign motions you defined in ICP:

- **Evergreen cash-cow campaigns.** You have no signal to lean on, so the lead magnet does the heavy lifting and has to earn the call cold. Lean on one strong, repeatable magnet (audit, analysis, sample) plus a clear value prop, run at scale. In a red-ocean market this magnet has to be creative to cut through; in a blue ocean you may need no magnet at all - the value prop is the bait.

- **Signal campaigns.** The trigger itself is half the pitch, so the magnet can be lighter or tied directly to the signal. "Saw the FTC filing - we'll run a compliance-gap audit on your exposure" fuses the signal and the magnet into one hyper-relevant offer. That fusion is why signal campaigns convert higher and justify hyper-personalized, multi-touch effort.

This is why you build different magnets per market and per motion, not one universal magnet. You'll assign each one to a specific playbook in Campaign Architecture (SOP 08).

## The market sophistication check

How much creativity the offer needs depends on market state - the same red/blue-ocean concept from ICP, applied to the offer layer, and it maps cleanly onto the Value Equation: in a blue ocean the dials are easy to max; in a red ocean you fight for every one.

### Blue ocean - simple wins

Underserved market, few competitors emailing, pain unaddressed. A direct value prop works ("we do X for Y without Z - want to learn more?"), and the offer itself is often the bait. 

Real example: a B2B affordable-housing certification software. 

Almost nobody was emailing this niche about this problem and no real solution existed. A 4-sentence cold email, direct value prop - 20% positive reply rate, $1.2M in contracts in 90 days. 

A fancy magnet would only have added friction.

### Red ocean - creativity required

Saturated market, every prospect getting 30 cold emails a week. 

You need a creative angle (a specific mechanism, not a generic value prop), a custom magnet that's hard to copy, and sharp differentiation upfront.

Real example: a B2B compliance-tech SaaS (dozens of competitors) built a "compliance audit" delivered live, walking the prospect through their 3 biggest exposure points. 3% positive reply rate (lower than blue ocean) but 60% lead-to-appointment, because the audit was so specific that anyone who replied was already qualified - closing enterprise deals and 200+ demos in 90 days. 

The audit spiked perceived likelihood and cut the prospect's effort to near zero.

### How to know which ocean you're in

- Search your value prop on Google and Claude. Page 1 full of competitors saying the same thing → red ocean. Sparse or generic → blue ocean.

- Ask 3 prospects how many cold emails they get a week. 10+ → red ocean. <5 → blue ocean.

- Check competitor outbound and ads. Looks like yours → red ocean. Can't find competitors → blue ocean.

- Put your ICP you identify in SOP 6 alongside your service/offering into Claude. It will tell you which market you are in!

## How this feeds the campaigns and the copy

The offer and lead magnet drive everything downstream.

First they define your campaign portfolio - which playbooks make sense - in Campaign Architecture. 

Then they drive the copy: if the offer is dialed and the magnet is concrete, copy practically writes itself - you describe the offer, name the magnet, ask for the call.

This is why most "my cold email isn't converting" problems are actually offer problems wearing a copy mask. 

The copy is usually fine; the thing being pitched isn't compelling, because three of the four Value Equation dials are sitting untouched. 

Diagnose at the offer layer first.

## Common offer mistakes

- **No lead magnet at all.** Pitching the core offer cold. Works only in true blue ocean.

- **Generic lead magnet.** "Free guide to outbound" in a market where everyone has one. Custom beats automated in saturated markets.

- **Delivering the magnet by email instead of on a call.** Now they read it and don't book. Force the call by making delivery live-only when possible. Unless you have automated the ability to create something custom at scale.

- **Skipping the market sophistication check.** Elaborate red-ocean offer in a blue-ocean market (overkill), or a bare blue-ocean offer in a red-ocean market (ignored).

- **One offer for both campaign types.** Using the same magnet for evergreen and signal instead of flexing it to the motion. The signal campaign should fuse the magnet to the trigger.

- **Vague dream outcome.** "We help you grow." Name the specific result and the number.

- **A high-effort offer.** Handing the prospect the work instead of removing it. Every step you make them do lowers your price and conversion.

- **No risk reversal.** Asking a cold prospect to believe you with nothing on the line. Put a guarantee on it and watch the stall rate drop.

- **A magnet that doesn't lead anywhere.** Valuable but it doesn't surface the bigger problem your core offer solves, so they take the free thing and walk.

## Action items

- Stress-test your core offer against the three gate tests (real pain / right price / specific enough to picture).

- Run the offer through the Value Equation. For each dial, write one concrete change.

- Add a risk reversal you can actually survive paying out. Write the guarantee in one sentence. (Optional.)

- Confirm red ocean or blue ocean from the three checks.

- Pick a lead magnet type and the job it does (reveal a problem / sample / one step), then run it through the Value Equation.

- Decide your magnet per campaign type: a creative magnet for evergreen (or none, in blue ocean) and a signal-fused magnet for signal campaigns.

- Write the lead magnet in one sentence. If you can't articulate what you're delivering and why it's worth 30 minutes, it isn't real yet.

When you've done all of it, you have a sellable, irresistible offer + lead magnet pair, split by campaign type. Now the campaigns can be designed around it.
