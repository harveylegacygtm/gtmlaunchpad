# 02 — Foundations: OIP Sequencing

**The Launchpad Playbook** • Module: Foundations • Reading time: 4 min

**TL;DR**

- The order is **Outbound → Inbound → Paid.** Each channel teaches you something the next one needs before it'll work.

- Outbound first because it's the fastest learning loop in B2B — 50 real conversations in weeks, not the 6–18 months inbound takes to compound.

- Outbound never turns off. Even at scale, it's the floor that keeps the lights on when other channels dip.

## The order

Build outbound first. Layer in inbound second. Add paid last.

Each channel earns the next:

- **Outbound** teaches you who your real customer is, what they actually struggle with, and what messaging gets them to respond. You learn through hundreds of real conversations in weeks.

- **Inbound** scales those lessons. You take what converted in outbound and turn it into content that compounds. Inbound *without* outbound first is guessing.

- **Paid** scales further once both work. You spend money to put proven message in front of more right people. Paid *without* the first two is gambling.

This isn't dogma. It's the order that minimizes wasted runway and maximizes the rate at which you learn.

## Why outbound first

**You need conversations, fast.** Until you've talked to 50+ prospects in your target market, your ICP and offer are hypothesis. Outbound forces real conversations within weeks. The information density is enormous — you learn what language they use, what objections come up, where your offer is mispriced, who your real competitors are, and what the buying process actually looks like.

**You need pipeline you control.** Inbound and paid are pull channels — they respond to demand that already exists. Outbound is push. It works whether or not the prospect knew they had your problem this morning. You wake demand up. You also control the volume dial: turn it up when you need pipeline, down when capacity is tight.

**You build the assets you'll use later.** Every outbound campaign generates raw material for inbound and paid: case studies, testimonials, prospect language for ad copy, objection patterns for FAQ content, sales-call recordings for podcast topics. When you eventually build inbound and paid, you're translating known-converting messaging into new channels — not starting from scratch.

## Why inbound second

Inbound rewards specificity. The content that converts speaks directly to a specific pain, in the specific language of a specific buyer, with specific proof points. You can't write that without first having had 50 conversations. Founders who try to write inbound from day one produce generic content because their understanding is generic.

Inbound also takes time to compound. SEO ranks in 6–18 months. YouTube and LinkedIn audiences build over months or years. Outbound pays the bills while inbound bakes.

What inbound gives you back: **trust at scale.** Outbound earns one prospect's trust at a time. Inbound earns it at the audience level — when prospects know your perspective and case studies before the call, the call gets easier and shorter. Inbound makes outbound calls warmer.

Right time to add inbound: ~3–6 months after starting outbound, when you have predictable pipeline, shareable case studies, and capacity to commit to a content cadence for at least six months.

## Why paid last

Paid is the most expensive channel per impression. The auction bids you against everyone else trying to reach your ICP. If your offer doesn't convert above the median competitor, you lose every auction. If your messaging is generic, your CTR is low, your quality scores tank, and you pay even more for fewer clicks.

Paid only works when you have: an offer that converts above median, messaging proven to convert, a funnel that converts cold traffic, case studies for trust, and working retargeting infrastructure. All of that gets built during the outbound and inbound phases.

Right time: ~12–24 months in. Some businesses never need paid — outbound + inbound is enough.

## The 4-stage timeline

| **Stage** | **Months** | **Outbound** | **Inbound** | **Paid** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0–6 | **Full intensity** — build infra, define ICP, write copy, generate first deals | — | — |
| 2 | 6–12 | Continues at full intensity | **Starting** — translate winning outbound messaging to LinkedIn, podcast, blog | — |
| 3 | 12–18 | Continues | **Producing** — warm leads from content, calls get easier | **Testing** — small spend to validate creative |
| 4 | 18+ | The floor — predictable, on-demand | The brand — warm leads, lower CAC | The lever — scale spend on proven creative |

## Mistakes that break the sequence

**Starting with paid because ****"****it scales fastest.****"** Paid scales budget fastest, not pipeline. Without proven messaging, paid scales waste. Founders who go paid-first burn thousands before realizing the offer wasn't ready.

**Adding inbound or paid before outbound is dialed.** Switching focus before outbound is a working system means you never fully learn outbound — and you don't have the proof points that make the others work. Finish what you start.

**Stopping outbound once inbound starts working.** Outbound is the floor. Even at $10M+ revenue it keeps running, because if inbound dips for a quarter (algorithm change, content team turnover, market shift), outbound keeps the lights on. Don't ever turn it off.
