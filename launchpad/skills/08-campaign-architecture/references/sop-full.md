## Where this fits

This is where it all comes together. ICP gave you who to email, the signals that say they're ready, and the two campaign types. Offer gave you what you pitch and the free thing that earns the call. This doc turns all of that into a real set of campaigns.

It comes before copy on purpose. You write copy one campaign at a time, so the campaigns have to exist first. Set the lineup here. Write the copy next. Build the list after that. Running and testing them is its own doc.

## What a campaign actually is

Most people think a campaign is "an email I sent." Wrong. A campaign is four things locked together and sent to enough people to learn what works:

- **ICP segment** - one slice of your market (mid-market SaaS, Series A startups, brokerages with 50+ agents).

- **Offer** - one thing you're pitching.

- **Persona** - one job title (CRO, Head of RevOps, Operations Manager).

- **Playbook** - one angle (a case study, a hiring trigger, a custom value prop).

Change any one of these between emails and you're really running two campaigns under one name. Now the data can't tell you anything. So lock all four. Send 1,000+ people the same thing. Read the results. Then change one thing and test again.

## The two campaign types

Every campaign is one of two kinds. These two connect ICP, offer, copy, and list. The playbooks below are just these two buckets, filled in.

- **Evergreen cash-cow.** Broad list, no signal to lean on. The offer and the free thing do the work, and the copy has to land cold. Cheap to build. Runs forever. Fewer replies per email. This pays the bills.

- **Signal.** The people showing a buying trigger. The trigger is half the pitch. The copy is very personal, and your best leads get hit on email, LinkedIn, and a call. Costs more to build. Doesn't scale as far. Way more replies. This boosts your numbers.

Run both. Don't pick one. The cash cow funds everything. The signal campaigns lift your average. Here's how the playbooks sort:

| **Playbook** | **Type** | **What it leans on** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Competitors / Page Followers | Signal | Followers of a competitor or a page with your audience |
| Lookalike Case Study | Evergreen | Your case studies + lookalike companies |
| Lead Magnet Testing | Evergreen | The free thing as the bait |
| Customized Value Prop | Hybrid | Light AI personalization across a broad list |
| Headcount Growth / Decline | Signal | Hiring-speed data |
| New In Role | Signal | Job-change data (under 90 days) |
| Funding / Jobs / Tech / Ads / etc. | Signal | The specific trigger (see the bench below) |

## Why you can't blend campaigns

Two reasons.

- **Mix people and your message gets weak.** Mix mid-market and enterprise and your copy speaks to neither. A CRO and a Sales Ops Manager care about different things. Copy for both is copy for no one.

- **Mix people and you can't read your data.** 5,000 emails, 30 replies - from one group, or spread evenly? You can't tell. So you can't decide what to do next.

The fix: split them up. Same ICP, different job title → different campaign. Same ICP and title, different offer → different campaign. It's more work up front. But you learn faster, and your next round is sharper.

## The six default playbooks

Six campaigns every client should test. Each is a different angle on the same ICP.

### 1 - Lookalike Case Study (evergreen)

Target companies that look like your best clients, and name the case study right away. Pull 5–10 of your best closed deals. Find companies like them (same industry, size, tools, model). Lead with the result. People believe results from companies that look like them. Use this any time you have 1–2 real wins to point to.

"Saw {company} is a lot like {reference_client} in size and stack - we took them from {old result} to {new result} in {timeframe}."

### 2 - Competitors / Page Followers (signal)

Scrape the people who follow your competitors on LinkedIn. Or scrape any page whose followers match your audience. Then write an offer just for them.

The follow is the signal. They already raised their hand for this topic - you're not guessing they care, they told you. Pull the follower list, filter down to your ICP, and write copy tied to why they're there.

Real example (a Legacy GTM client): a client was raising money for a horror movie. We scraped the followers of "Bloody Disgusting" on LinkedIn - people who clearly love horror. We found the ones who also fit the ICP and reached out with an offer built for horror fans. The page did the filtering for us.

### 3 - Lead Magnet Testing (evergreen)

Keep the ICP, offer, and copy the same. Change only the free thing (free audit, free creative, free strategy, competitor analysis). Track how many calls each one books. The free thing is the bait that earns the call, and different bait works for different people. Run this once your core copy is working and you want more booked calls.

### 4 - Customized Value Prop (hybrid)

AI writes 1–2 custom lines per company - from their mission, their market, and the person's role. The rest of the email stays the same. Done well, every email feels personal. Done badly (generic AI compliments) it flops. The difference is the prompt and the data. This sits between evergreen and signal: a broad list with a light personal touch.

### 5 - Headcount Growth / Decline (signal)

Target by how fast a company is hiring. Growing fast (over 10% a year)? Pitch tools that help them scale. Cutting staff or doing layoffs? Pitch tools that cut cost or replace headcount. Both are buying - you just change the message. Great when your market has a mix of both.

### 6 - New In Role (signal)

Target people who started a new role in the last 90 days. That's the window where they swap vendors fastest. Open with a quick congrats, then a specific first-90-days offer. Run it in the background so it pulls fresh new-in-role people every week.

"Congrats on the new role at {company}. Most {role}s spend the first 90 days reviewing the stack - figured I'd save you a step."

## The signal bench

Beyond the six, these are all signal campaigns. Add one when your data says a certain trigger predicts who buys. Each maps to a Signal-to-Offer trigger from ICP and to an enrichment in List Building:

- **Tech stack** - target by the tools they use (BuiltWith). "Saw {company} runs Salesforce - we built a connector that…"

- **Funding** - recent rounds (Crunchbase). Fresh money = shopping for vendors.

- **Job postings** - roles they're hiring for (job-board scraping). Hiring an SDR = they need outbound.

- **Missing role** - companies without a role you replace (no CFO → fractional CFO; no Head of RevOps → your tool).

- **Following competitors / influencers** - people following a competitor, or anyone you can scrape who has your audience.

- **Running ads, traffic shifts, G2 / Maps reviews, social activity** - each one points to a change worth its own angle.

## How your offer decides your playbooks

Your lineup isn't a free-for-all. The offer and free thing from decide which playbooks are even worth running.

- **Blue ocean, simple offer** → a lean evergreen campaign carries it. You may not need a heavy free thing at all.

- **Red ocean, creative free thing** → Lead Magnet Testing and Lookalike Case Study do the heavy lifting. You need something hard to copy to stand out.

- **Signal-fused free thing** → the signal playbooks (New In Role, Headcount, Funding, etc.) ride the free thing you tied to the trigger ("saw the FTC filing - here's a compliance-gap audit").

Pick playbooks for the offer you actually have, not a wish list.

## Build your portfolio

Don't run one campaign and wait. Build a set: one evergreen base plus 2–3 signal playbooks against the same ICP.

## Test ICP first, then offer, then copy

When something isn't working, most people reach for the copy. Wrong move. Here's what actually matters most - and why the whole playbook is in this order:

- **Test the ICP.** Different groups reply at wildly different rates. Same offer, different ICP can 3–5x your results. Biggest impact.

- **Test the offer.** Different positioning and a different free thing convert differently. Second biggest - and most people skip it.

- **Test the copy.** Real, but it usually moves things 20–50%, not 3–5x. Smallest of the three.

This is also why the Diagnostic Framework in (SOP 11) checks things from the bottom up: most broken campaigns are broken above the copy. How to actually run and test them is in that same doc.

## Test the angle, not the words

When you do test copy, test the angle - not single words.

- **Wrong:** two emails that are 90% the same with one different sentence. That's just a copy variant inside one campaign, not a new angle.

- **Right:** totally different angles - one names a mechanism, one doesn't; one leads with a cost calculation, one leads with a client you'd recognize.

Eggs and flour, not icing. Test things that, if they win, would change how you write every email after.

## Volume decides how fast you learn

Each variant needs ~500–1,000 people to mean anything. More sending = more tests at once = faster answers. Volume isn't just about hitting your revenue goal - it's about how fast you learn. Once you find the winning angle, you can often dial volume back.

## Once it works

When a campaign hits the benchmark (roughly 1 positive reply per 50–100 people - this varies by niche), do two things:

- **Send more of the same.** Don't get clever. Scale the exact playbook that's working.

- **Copy it to a similar group.** Same structure, new ICP.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing personas or ICPs in one campaign.** Different language, different objections. Split them.

- **Running only one campaign type.** Only evergreen leaves replies on the table. Only signal won't scale and starves your pipeline. Run both.

- **Testing tiny word tweaks.** Won't move anything. Test eggs and flour.

- **Fixing copy when the problem is higher up.** Check ICP, then offer, then copy angles before you touch words.

- **Running one playbook at a time.** Each only reaches part of your market. Run 3–4 together.

- **Breaking a winner by over-tweaking it.** Once it works, scale it. Don't get cute.

- **Too few people per test.** Under 1,000 per variant is just noise.

## Action items

- Lock the four variables (ICP / offer / persona / playbook) for your first campaign.

- Decide your evergreen base and your signal layer. Confirm you're running both.

- From the six defaults, pick 2–3 to run together (default trio: Lookalike Case Study + Competitors / Page Followers + New In Role).

- Match each playbook to the offer/free thing it leans on and the trigger it needs.

- Check that your daily volume supports the number of tests you want to run.

When that's done, you have a set of campaigns - and a clear brief for the copy you write next.
