## Where this fits

The campaign's job was to earn a reply. This is what you do with it. This doc takes a raw inbound reply, turns it into a booked call, and keeps that booking warm until they show up ready to buy. It's the conversion layer: the difference between a campaign that "gets replies" and one that books and holds meetings. The two numbers it owns, LTA and show rate, are lagging metrics (SOP 11), and they're where most pipeline quietly leaks.

## The through-line: stay consistent with the cold email

Before any of the tactics below, lock in this one principle, because it runs through every section. Whatever angle, offer, and tone won the reply has to carry all the way through: the reply you send back, the booking conversation, the pre-call emails, and the call itself.

If your cold email led with a specific mechanism and a free audit in a casual, human voice, don't suddenly answer in stiff corporate-speak pitching a different package. The prospect raised their hand for a specific thing. Deliver that thing.

A bait-and-switch, even an accidental one in tone, breaks trust, drops your show rate, and wastes the most expensive lead you have. Consistency is just the offer continued past the inbox. The cold email is a promise. The rest of this doc is you keeping it.

## 1 — What to do after you get a reply

Think about what a reply costs: domains, inboxes, warmup, a list you qualified and enriched, an offer you sweated over, copy you rewrote five times, three weeks of sending. All of it exists to manufacture one thing: a human typing back. The reply is the payoff, and it's the rarest, most expensive event in the system. Treating it as a someday-when-I-get-to-it task is how people quietly torch their pipeline.

**Speed-to-lead is the single biggest lever.** Interest decays by the hour. Reply in five minutes and you catch them while they're still in their inbox thinking about you. Reply in two days and you're a stranger reintroducing yourself. Triage within minutes during working hours, not once a day. If you do nothing else in this doc, respond faster.

**Even the no's are gold.** A prospect who tells you "not interested, we already have a vendor" just handed you market intelligence you'd pay a research firm for. The money is in the mouth of the market. Log what they say and feed it into next month's copy and offer (SOP 09). Don't delete it. Mine it.

**The prime directive:** every reply gets driven toward a booked call with the least friction possible. That's the job. Everything below is how.

## 3 — Types of replies and how to handle them

Every cold reply sorts into one of seven buckets. Recognize the type, run the move, and keep the angle, offer, and tone consistent with the email that earned it.

**1. Interested / positive.** The win. Don't get fancy. Book the call while the iron's hot. Thank them, confirm the value in one line (the same value your cold email promised), and hand them the easiest possible path to a slot. Don't ask "what's your availability next week?" That's homework. Offer two concrete times or drop a booking link, and confirm. Then they flow into the pre-call experience in section 6.

**2. Objection / question.** "We already have a vendor." "Too expensive." "What's the price?" "Send me some info." These aren't no's. They're requests for a reason. Handle the objection, reframe toward value, and re-ask for the call. Common reframes:

- **"****We have a vendor.****"** Acknowledge it and ask how their current vendor is working out, whether they're happy. If not, ask if you can show how you'd plug the gap. Then offer the magnet anyway: *"**Makes sense. Even if you stay with them, I'll show you {{X}} on a quick 15-min call and you keep it either way.**"*

- **"****Too expensive / no budget.****"** Ask if it's a hard budget, then reframe to ROI: *"**If we got you {{result}}, would it be worth finding the budget?**"* If they still push back, leave it and follow up in a few months.

- **"****Send me info.****"** Send a short summary or a quick highlight, just enough to answer the question and spark curiosity, not a data dump. Then re-offer the call with a personalized reason tied to your cold email and lead magnet. Template:

Hey {{firstName}},

We help {{X}} do {{Y}} without {{Z}}, using {{mechanism}}.

Here's a quick look at how we got {{caseStudy}} {{result}}.

Would love to show you how {{companyName}} could do the same. Open at {{time1}} or {{time2}}? Or grab a slot here: {{link}}

Looking forward to it, {{signature}}

- **"****What's the price?****"** Give a range to qualify, then pull to the call for specifics.

**3. Referral / wrong person.** "I'm not the right person, talk to Dana." Gold. Thank them and ask for a warm intro: *"**Could you point me to Dana, or is it okay if I mention you suggested I reach out?**"* Treat the referral as near-warm. Internal referrals convert far better than cold.

**4. Not interested (a clean no).** A polite, reasoned no. Don't argue. Arguing burns goodwill and risks a spam complaint. Exit gracefully, leave the door open (*"**no worries, if {{X}} changes, I'm here**"*), and capture the reason. The reason is the data; the graceful exit protects your reputation.

**5. Not now / timing.** "Circle back in Q3." Real, common, and easy to fumble. Don't drop them and don't pester. Confirm the timeframe, set a concrete follow-up, and add them to a re-engagement track. Timing no's turn into yeses more than any other type, if you actually follow up.

**6. Unsubscribe / angry.** "Take me off your list." Remove them immediately, no argument. This is non-negotiable: user-reported spam is the #1 killer of deliverability (SOP 05), and the thresholds are brutally low. One graceful removal protects the inboxes that earn every other reply.

**7. Auto-reply / out-of-office.** Not a human, so don't treat it like one. Pull the contact out of the active flow and re-queue for after the return date. Sendkit's inbox flags these for you.

## 4 — Following up when the meeting isn't booked

Plenty of replies are warm but don't book on the first exchange, and plenty of leads go quiet mid-conversation. The follow-up is where a lot of meetings actually get made. Most people give up one message too early. Because these people already engaged, you've earned a few more touches than a cold sequence. Just keep them light and stay detached (needy "just checking in" repels). Four moves:

- **Add value, don't chase.** Every follow-up gives something: a relevant case study, a quick insight, a resource, a one-line teardown of something on their site. "Did you see my email?" is not a follow-up. It's a nag.

- **Personalize it.** Reference something specific: a recent post, a company change, the exact pain they mentioned. A copy-pasted bump tells them they're one of a thousand, so they don't bother.

- **Make it human and a little funny.** A bit of humor breaks up a stiff inbox and makes you memorable. A light, self-aware line (*"**I promise I'm not a bot, though that's exactly what a bot would say**"*) lands far better than another formal nudge.

- **Use a meme, sparingly.** A well-chosen, on-topic meme or GIF in a late follow-up is a proven pattern-interrupt. It reads as a human having fun, not a sequence firing. One, on-brand, never forced, and never with a prospect whose tone clearly won't take it.

Through all of it, keep the angle consistent with the original email. You're re-offering the same thing in a fresh wrapper, not pitching something new. Space the touches out, stay useful, and give them an easy yes every time.

## 5 — Stack channels: warm calling + LinkedIn (2–3x your LTA)

Email alone leaves money on the table. Once someone replies, or once you've spotted a high-intent lead, layering in a warm call and a LinkedIn touch can double or triple your lead-to-appointment ratio, because familiarity compounds across channels. Three or four touches from a name they now recognize beats ten emails from a stranger.

- **Warm calling.** These aren't cold calls. The person already engaged by email. A quick call to book the time directly converts far better than another email round-trip, and it's the fastest path from "interested" to "on the calendar."

- **LinkedIn connection + message.** Send a connection request and a short message that echoes the email angle. Now you're in their inbox and their feed, and you have a face, which lifts both LTA and show rate.

- **Have the right person connect.** Whoever runs the call (the SDR, or better, the closer or founder) should be the one connecting on LinkedIn and doing the warm touches. It builds the relationship with the person they'll actually meet, not a faceless inbox.

Sequence it: reply → quick call attempt + LinkedIn connect within the day → book. The same multi-channel stack also powers the booking confirmations in section 6.

## 6 — When a meeting is booked: get them to show

The booking is not the win. Between "yes" and the call, your prospect has a hundred competing priorities and you're the least important one. By call day you might be the last thing on their calendar. People don't no-show because they hate you. They no-show because they forgot you, or the spark that made them book cooled and nothing reheated it. So you keep adding wood to the fire. Five things, run on every booking:

- **Confirmation, in the thread and in the app.** Confirm twice. First, reply right in the conversation thread (*"**locked in for Thursday 2pm, here's what we'll cover**"*) so the human-to-human thread shows the booking. Second, the scheduling app sends its own confirmation and a calendar invite with a real agenda, never "30 min chat." Two confirmations, two surfaces, zero "wait, did that book?"

- **A strong thank-you page.** Redirect every booking to a page that leads with a short founder video (founder, not a junior rep) answering who you are, what you do, how you're different, and the proof. Below it: social proof in text and image (logos, results, a short case study) plus links to read or watch more. (Field tip: ask your clients why they hired you and use their words.)

- **A manual LinkedIn + SMS + call touch from the person running the call.** Automation confirms; a human convinces. The person actually taking the call sends a personal LinkedIn message, a short SMS, and ideally a quick call or voice note: *"**Looking forward to Thursday, here's the one thing I want to make sure we cover.**"* A real touch from a real face is the single strongest show-rate lever you have.

- **An SMS reminder.** The day before and/or the morning of. Texts get read when emails don't. Keep it short and pattern-interrupting, with the time and a one-click reschedule link, not a robotic "reminder: your appointment is at 2pm."

- **A pre-call email flow that adds value and context.** Two or three touches across the gap, each doing one job: set the frame and "what to expect," drop one piece of social proof or a case study, and pre-handle the top objection (price, vendor, timing) so it's not a landmine live. Every touch restates the date and time. By call day they know you, trust you, remember why they booked, and their main objection is already softened, so the call is a warm continuation, not a cold restart, and the lead magnet you promised gets delivered live.

### Recover the no-shows

A no-show is usually a forgotten lead, not a dead one. Within the hour: a no-judgment *"**looks like we missed each other, grab a new time here**"* with the reschedule link. Day 2 and day 4: two more touches restating the value, new angle, same offer. Then back to a longer re-engagement track if they don't rebook. Worked consistently, recovery claws back 20–30% of no-shows, pipeline you already paid for. And put a one-click reschedule link in every confirmation and reminder: a reschedule is a kept relationship; a no-show with no path back is a dead one.

## 7 — Limiting no-shows

Two levers, in order of leverage:

- **Run the five steps above.** The pre-call experience IS the no-show fix. Most operators send one ignored automated reminder and wonder why 40% vanish. Confirmations on two surfaces, a thank-you page, a manual human touch, an SMS, and a value-adding pre-call flow, stacked, is what turns a 50% show rate into 70%+.

- **Shorten the booking window. Cap it at 4 days, ideally book within 24–48 hours.** This is the most underrated structural lever in the whole doc. The longer the gap between booking and call, the more time interest has to cool and competing priorities have to pile up. Set your scheduler to only show near-term slots so prospects can't book three weeks out. Less waiting, less forgetting, higher show rate, for free.

## Common mistakes

- **Slow replies.** The #1 LTA killer. Minutes matter; days are fatal.

- **Switching angle or tone after the reply.** The cold email made a promise. Keep it through the call.

- **Treating the booking as the finish line.** It's the start of a new sequence, not the end of one.

- **One generic reminder and nothing else.** That's a coin flip, not a pre-call experience.

- **A junior face, or no face, in the video and confirmations.** The person running the call should be visible. It builds trust the fastest.

- **Letting prospects book three weeks out.** You're handing interest time to die. Cap the window.

- **Arguing with no's or ignoring unsubscribes.** You won't win the argument, and a spam complaint hurts every inbox.

- **Single-channel.** Email-only leaves your LTA at half what warm calls + LinkedIn would get you.
