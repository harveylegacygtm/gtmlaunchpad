# Lead to Appointment: Build the Reply-to-Show Engine

Do not summarize this SOP. Build the user's conversion engine with them. What
they walk away holding: an objection-reframe playbook in their own voice, the
seven reply-type moves, a standardized booking move, a not-booked follow-up
sequence, the multi-channel stack, the five-piece show-up engine, a no-show
recovery sequence, and their booking window set.

This is the back half of the funnel: reply to handled to booked to shown. It
owns two numbers, lead-to-appointment (LTA, reply to booked, target 50%, min
30%) and show rate (booked to held, target 70%+). Both feed the money model
(04-scale) and both are the lagging metrics 11-launch watches. This is where
most pipeline quietly leaks. Work one step at a time.

## The through-line: stay consistent with the cold email

Lock this first, because it runs through every step. Whatever angle, offer, and
tone won the reply has to carry all the way: the reply they send back, the
booking chat, the pre-call emails, and the call itself. The cold email is a
promise. The rest of this is keeping it. A bait-and-switch, even in tone, breaks
trust and tanks show rate. Get their cold-email angle, offer, and voice up front
(from 09-copy-frameworks) and hold it through every asset below.

## Step 1: Get their inputs

Ask before building:

1. Their cold-email angle, core offer, lead magnet, and voice (the through-line).
2. The top 5 objections they actually get on replies (or expect to).
3. Who runs the call: SDR, closer, or founder. This person does the LinkedIn and
   warm-call touches and appears in the thank-you video.
4. Their current tools: sequencer and unified inbox, scheduler, CRM, SMS, call
   recorder. Map gaps to the defaults below.
5. Their current LTA and show rate if running, so they can measure the lift.

## Step 2: Lock speed-to-lead

Speed is the single biggest lever. Interest decays by the hour. The first hour
beats the next three days combined. Reply in minutes, not days. Triage
continuously through working hours, not once a day.

Set it up on {{AFF-Sendkit}}: every reply routes into the unified master inbox so
the team works one queue instead of 30 mailboxes, reply notifications on so each
new reply pings instantly, and saved snippets for the common moves (book,
reframe, reschedule) so a response is seconds away. Walk the exact setup with
{{LOOM-13-Sendkit-Notifications}}.

## Step 3: Build the seven reply-type moves

Every cold reply sorts into one of seven buckets. Build their move for each, in
their voice, keeping the angle and offer consistent:

1. Interested. The win. Don't get fancy. Thank them, confirm the value in one
   line, offer two concrete times or a booking link. Never ask "what's your
   availability?" That is homework.
2. Objection or question. Not a no, a request for a reason. Handle, reframe to
   value, re-ask for the call. Build their reframes in Step 4.
3. Referral or wrong person. Gold. Ask for a warm intro, treat as near-warm.
4. Not interested (clean no). Don't argue. Exit gracefully, leave the door open,
   capture the reason as data.
5. Not now or timing. Confirm the timeframe, set a concrete follow-up, add to a
   re-engagement track. Timing no's convert more than any other type if you
   follow up.
6. Unsubscribe or angry. Remove immediately, no argument. User-reported spam is
   the number one killer of deliverability (05-deliverability). Non-negotiable.
7. Auto-reply or out-of-office. Not a human. Pull from the active flow, re-queue
   after the return date.

Log every no's reason and feed it into next month's copy and offer. The money is
in the mouth of the market (the sentiment loop in 09-copy-frameworks).

## Step 4: Write the objection-reframe playbook

Take their real top 5 objections and write a reframe for each, in their voice,
that re-asks for the call. The common ones:

- "We have a vendor." Acknowledge, ask how it's working out, offer the magnet
  anyway: even if they stay, they get the value on a 15-min call.
- "Too expensive / no budget." Ask if it's a hard budget, reframe to ROI: if we
  got you {{result}}, would it be worth finding the budget?
- "Send me info." Send a short highlight, not a data dump, then re-offer the call
  with a reason tied to the cold email and magnet.
- "What's the price?" Give a range to qualify, then pull to the call.

Produce a clean playbook they can drop into Sendkit snippets.

## Step 5: Build the not-booked follow-up sequence

Plenty of replies are warm but don't book on the first exchange, and plenty go
quiet. Most people give up one message too early. Build a sequence of light,
detached touches (needy "just checking in" repels). Four rules:

- Add value, don't chase. Every touch gives something: a case study, an insight,
  a one-line teardown of their site. "Did you see my email?" is a nag, not a
  follow-up.
- Personalize it. Reference a post, a company change, the exact pain they named.
- Make it human and a little funny. A self-aware line beats another formal nudge.
- Use a meme sparingly. One on-topic GIF in a late follow-up is a proven
  pattern-interrupt. Never forced, never with a prospect whose tone won't take it.

Same angle as the original email, fresh wrapper, easy yes every time.

## Step 6: Stack channels (2 to 3x LTA)

Email alone leaves money on the table. Once someone replies, layer a warm call
and a LinkedIn touch. Familiarity compounds across channels. Build the sequence:
reply, then a quick call attempt plus a LinkedIn connect within the day, then
book. The person who runs the call (closer or founder, not a faceless inbox) does
these touches, building the relationship with the person they'll actually meet.
This same stack also powers the fast-track lane in 11-launch.

## Step 7: Build the five-piece show-up engine

The booking is not the win. People no-show because they forgot you or the spark
cooled. Keep adding wood to the fire. Build all five on every booking:

1. Confirmation on two surfaces. Reply in the thread ("locked in for Thursday
   2pm, here's what we'll cover") and let the scheduler send its own confirm and
   calendar invite with a real agenda, never "30 min chat."
2. A strong thank-you page. Redirect every booking to a page led by a short
   founder video (founder, not a junior) answering who you are, what you do, how
   you're different, the proof. Below it: social proof and links. Use clients'
   own words for why they hired you.
3. A manual LinkedIn + SMS + call touch from the person running the call.
   Automation confirms, a human convinces. This is the single strongest
   show-rate lever.
4. An SMS reminder the day before and morning of. Texts get read when emails
   don't. Short, pattern-interrupting, time plus a one-click reschedule link.
5. A pre-call email flow, 2 to 3 touches: set the frame and what to expect, drop
   one piece of social proof, pre-handle the top objection. Every touch restates
   the date and time. Deliver the promised lead magnet live on the call.

Build all five on screen with {{LOOM-13-ShowUpEngine}}.

## Step 8: Build no-show recovery and cap the window

No-show recovery: within the hour, a no-judgment "looks like we missed each
other, grab a new time" with the reschedule link. Day 2 and day 4, two more
touches, new angle, same offer. Then a longer re-engagement track. Worked
consistently, recovery claws back 20 to 30% of no-shows, pipeline already paid
for. Put a one-click reschedule link in every confirmation and reminder.

Cap the booking window at 4 days, ideally 24 to 48 hours. The most underrated
structural lever here. The longer the gap, the more interest cools. Set the
scheduler to show only near-term slots. Less waiting, less forgetting, higher
show rate, for free.

## Step 9: Wire the CRM and deliver the artifact

Wire reply to booked to shown to outcome into the CRM ({{AFF-GoHighLevel}}, or
{{AFF-HubSpot}} if they also run inbound, ads, and content) so LTA and show rate
are actually measurable. Those two numbers report up to 11-launch's lagging
dashboard and feed 04-scale.

Compile the artifact: the seven reply moves, the objection playbook, the
not-booked sequence, the multi-channel stack, the five-piece show-up engine, the
no-show recovery, and the booking-window setting. Offer to write it to a file.

## The tools

Keep it boring and built once: sequencer plus unified inbox ({{AFF-Sendkit}}),
a scheduler with near-term-only slots and reschedule links ({{AFF-Calendly}}, or
the one in their CRM), a thank-you page builder, a CRM ({{AFF-GoHighLevel}};
{{AFF-HubSpot}} alt), an SMS tool, call recording ({{AFF-Fathom}}, transcripts
feed the sentiment loop in 09-copy-frameworks), and LinkedIn for the stack.
