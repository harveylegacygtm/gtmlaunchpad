## What you'll get from this SOP

By the end of this SOP you'll be able to say one sentence most founders can't: **"****I need to send X emails a month to make $Y - and it costs me about $Z.****"** That sentence is the line between running a *system* and running a *guess*.

This is the **S in SCOPE - the math layer.** Build it once and every other lever (copy, offer, list, deliverability) has a number to move. You'll model the funnel backward from a revenue goal, pressure-test whether the volume is realistic, and see a ballpark cost to hit it.

**Two ways to use the model:**

- **Never run cold outbound?** The model gives you a benchmark drawn from proven outbound systems, so you know what to aim for before you start.

- **Already running it?** Once the model is consistent across campaigns, you forecast growth against it. It's a planning tool, not just a setup tool.

You'll likely build different versions for different ICPs - your SMB motion has different ACVs, conversion rates, and volume needs than your Enterprise motion. The calculator handles this with side-by-side columns so you can compare them.

## The funnel - six stages, in order

Every cold outbound system runs through the same funnel:

- **Emails sent** - total volume going out the door

- **Positive replies** - people who actually want to keep talking

- **Calls booked** - positive replies that converted to a calendar slot

- **Calls held** - booked calls that showed up

- **Qualified calls** - held calls that are the right person, right need, right timing

- **Deals closed** - qualified calls that became revenue

Each stage has a conversion rate to the next. Multiply them together and you get your end-to-end yield: revenue per email sent, and exactly how many calls you need to book to hit your target.

The whole point: answer the only question that matters when scaling - **if I want to make $X next quarter, how many emails do I need to send?**

Most playbooks split the funnel into "emails sent → replied → replied positively." That math is fine, but it creates two metrics that move together and overcomplicate things.

We use one ratio for the funnel math: **leads contacted per positive reply.**

Example: 500 positive replies × 100 leads-per-positive = 5,000 leads contacted to generate them.

This single number captures funnel efficiency. Reply rate alone misleads - some niches reply at 1.5% but nearly all positive (great ratio); others reply at 4% but mostly "wrong person" or angry (poor ratio). The combined ratio gives you the truth.

**Workshop benchmark: 1 positive reply per 50–100 contacts.** That's the zip code you want. Better than 1-per-50 means you've got something special. A more competitive niche means contacting more leads. The better your offer and copy - and the less saturated the niche - the fewer people you need to contact to get a yes.

**Reply rate and positive reply rate stay useful - as diagnostic signals, not math drivers.** When a campaign breaks, knowing *which* dropped tells you what to fix. The calculator tracks both as diagnostic-only inputs (yellow cells).

## The benchmarks

Starting reference points. They vary by industry - some niches reply less but convert more (real estate), some the reverse. Not gospel. A "you're in the right zip code" check.

| **Stage** | **Healthy range** | **What to do if below** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leads contacted per positive reply | **1 per 50–100** (primary efficiency metric) | Diagnose offer/copy fit and ICP precision |
| Reply rate (diagnostic) | 2%+ minimum | Diagnose deliverability, list quality, copy - see **SOP 05** |
| Positive reply rate (diagnostic, of replies) | 15–20%+ | Diagnose offer/copy fit and ICP precision |
| Lead-to-Appointment ratio (LTA) | 30–50%, ideal 50% | Diagnose your reply handling - see **SOP 13** |
| Show rate | 70%+ (cold shows lower than inbound; keep above 60% with proper pre-call nurture) | Diagnose pre-call nurture - see **SOP 13** |
| Qualification rate | 80%+ | Targeting needs work. Score leads and focus on higher-score ones |
| Close rate (qualified → deals) | Industry-dependent - usually 20% | Sales-process problem, outside this playbook |
| Qualified/total calls to deals | Industry-dependent | Audit your sales and closing process |

A "good campaign" by our definition: **1 positive lead for every 50–100 contacts.** If you've got that, you don't have a campaign problem - you have a volume problem. Send more.

## How to build your model

Pick the goal. Work backward.

**Step 1 - Pick your revenue target for the time period.** Default 90 days. Not your annual goal. Not your stretch fantasy. Your specific 90-day number.

**Step 2 - Calculate average contract value (ACV).** Average dollar value of a closed deal. If you have data, use it. If not (early stage), use your offer's stated price.

**Step 3 - Plug in conversion rates and the leads-per-positive ratio.** Real numbers if you have them. If not, start with workshop benchmarks:

- Close rate (qualified → deals): 25%

- Qualification rate (held → qualified): 80%

- Show rate (booked → held): 70%

- LTA (positive reply → booked): 30%

- Leads contacted per positive reply: 75

**Step 4 - Work backward through the funnel.**

Revenue target ÷ ACV                    = deals needed

Deals needed ÷ close rate               = qualified calls needed

Qualified calls ÷ qualification rate    = held calls needed

Held calls ÷ show rate                  = booked calls needed

Booked calls ÷ LTA                      = positive replies needed

Positive replies × leads-per-positive   = leads to contact

Leads to contact × sequence length × 0.85 = total emails to send

Total emails ÷ months in period         = monthly emails to send

The 0.85 multiplier accounts for leads that don't get the full sequence - some convert on email 1, some unsubscribe, some bounce. Across thousands of leads it averages to ~85% of theoretical max.

**Step 5 - Sanity-check the volume.** Is the total realistic for your budget and timeline? If not, three options: improve the funnel (better targeting/offer/copy → fewer leads per positive), increase volume (more sending capacity - covered in SOP 05), or adjust the goal. Most people pick option 3 first, then 1 and 2 as the system matures.

## Worked example - $50K in 90 days

You sell B2B SaaS at $10,000 ACV. You want $50,000 in new revenue over 90 days.

$50,000 ÷ $10,000 ACV          = 5 deals needed

5 deals ÷ 25% close rate       = 20 qualified calls needed

20 qualified ÷ 80% qual        = 25 held calls needed

25 held ÷ 70% show             = 36 booked calls needed

36 booked ÷ 30% LTA            = 120 positive replies needed

120 positive × 75 ratio        = 9,000 leads to contact

9,000 leads × 3 × 0.85         = 22,950 emails over 90 days

22,950 ÷ 3 months              = 7,650 emails / month  (~348/day on 22 sending days)

That's a model. Now you have something to optimize, scale, and forecast against.

## What it costs - a quick example

You can't model revenue without a sense of infrastructure cost. Here's a *ballpark* using the simplest possible setup - **Google-only** - for the $50K example above (7,650 emails/month ≈ 348/day across 22 sending days):

- Google sends at 15 emails/inbox/day → **~24 inboxes**

- 3 inboxes per domain → **~8 domains**

- Inboxes: 24 × ~$3.50 ≈ **$84/mo** · Domains (cheap, amortized): ≈ **$9/mo**

- **Raw sending infrastructure ≈ $93/month.** Software and data stack on top - Sendkit (~$97), Clay (~$446), and data (~$77) bring the monthly total to **~$713 with cheap domains**. The calculator totals it for you.

**⏸ Before you configure real infrastructure - read SOP 05.** Which provider you send through (**Google, Microsoft 365, or Azure**) and how you split your volume is a *deliverability* decision, not a math one - and it changes your cost. SOP 05 covers all three and helps you pick your mix. **Then** come back and run the Infra Calculator tab with your real split.

## The volume rule - please respect it

If you take one thing from this SOP, take this:

**The number to remember: about 1,000 leads contacted per campaign before the data means anything.**

Cold outbound is a law-of-averages game. Flip a coin twice and get heads twice - is it a two-headed coin? No. Flip it enough times and you'll see the real ratio. Same logic here. Data only becomes meaningful at volume.

When someone says "my cold email isn't working" and we look at their dashboard, the most common answer is they contacted 400 leads and gave up. That's not a campaign - that's a sample.

- **The standard: about 1,000 leads contacted per campaign** before you judge it. At a 1-per-75 ratio that is roughly 13 positive replies, enough to read a real pattern. Fewer than that is a sample, not a signal.

If your true positive-reply rate is 1 per 75, you need enough contacts to statistically expect 10 or more positives. At 75 contacts you'd expect 1, no signal. At 1,000 you'd expect about 13, now you have a picture. Send enough. Then judge.

## What to do once you have a winning model

Two moves, in order:

**Move 1 - Scale volume on the same playbook.** If you're hitting the benchmarks (1 per 75), don't get clever. Don't add variables. Send more of the same. Most people fail here by optimizing a winning campaign instead of duplicating it.

**Move 2 - Mirror the winning strategy across adjacent segments.** Once you've maxed your TAM or sending capacity for the original segment, take the offer + messaging that worked and try it on a similar segment. New control, same playbook. Or retarget the same segment every 1–2 months with fresh copy.

Example: a winning campaign targeting Series A B2B SaaS founders in marketing automation. You've contacted everyone in that TAM at your volume cap. Mirror it to Series A founders in adjacent verticals (sales tech, customer-success tech, RevOps). Same offer, same shape, different list.

You're after repeatable wins, not the one perfect campaign. Three good campaigns at scale beats one great campaign run once.

## Common mistakes that break the money model

**Mistake 1 - Optimizing too early.** You send 500 emails, get 1 reply, panic, change everything, reset your data, learn nothing. Send the volume first. Read the data second. Change one thing third. Minimum 500–1,000 leads before optimizing.

**Mistake 2 - Confusing reply rate with positive reply rate.** Reply rate includes "stop emailing me," OOO, and "wrong person." Positive reply rate is the one that matters for revenue. Track both as diagnostics; use leads-per-positive as your primary number.

**Mistake 3 - Not tracking LTA.** You celebrate 50 positive replies, then realize 8 booked calls. That's not a lead-gen problem - it's reply handling. Track LTA and you'll find the leak fast. (See **SOP 13**.)

**Mistake 4 - Underestimating volume needed.** You decide you need 20 deals but forget close rate (25%), qual rate (80%), show rate (70%). You actually need ~143 booked calls → ~477 positive replies → ~35,800 contacts → ~91,000 emails. Always do the full backward math. The calculator does it for you - use it.

**Mistake 5 - Treating the model as static.** Numbers change as you optimize. Re-run monthly. As offer, copy, and ICP improve, leads-per-positive drops; as reply handling improves, LTA climbs. The model should get more efficient over time. If it's not, something's wrong.
