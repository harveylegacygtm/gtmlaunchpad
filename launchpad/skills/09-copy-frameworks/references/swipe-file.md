# Copy Swipe File: Templates, Openers, Subjects, CTAs

This is the working reference for the 09-copy-frameworks skill. Pull from it
while you draft with the user. Do not read it to them. Use it to draft fast,
then shape every line to their ICP, offer, and the real prospect they picked.

Rules that still apply to everything here:

- Match the customer's words from the 06-market-research skill. These templates
  are skeletons, not finished copy.
- 40 to 80 words. Plain text. One CTA. Grade 5 to 6.
- Merge fields use single braces: {first_name}, {company}, {outcome},
  {no_good_thing}, {case_study}. Fill with real data or a placeholder.
- Never invent proof. Use {CLIENT_RESULT} or {STAT_NEEDED} until they give you a
  real number.
- These are the building blocks. The skill still leads: write the Dream Email
  first, then reach for a template that fits.

## First-touch templates (email 1)

Each one is a different angle, not a different wording. Pick the one that fits
the campaign type and the data the user actually has. Test 3 to 4 against each
other.

### 1. Poke the bear (Josh Braun style)

Best for red oceans where you have a sharp read on their pain.

```
{first_name} - {relevant first line}

{poke the bear question about how they handle the problem today}

We're helping companies like {case_study} hit {outcome} in {timeframe}
without {no_good_thing}.

Worth a look?
```

### 2. Lead magnet (give before you ask)

The strongest type when the offer has a real free thing. Pull the free thing
from the 07-offer skill.

```
{first_name} - I saw {something specific and relevant}.

We help companies hit {result}. But I'd never expect you to take my word for it.

So I made you a {free thing}. Mind if I send it over, and you tell me if it's
useful?
```

### 3. Extremely short (one line)

For huge markets and offers people either want or don't.

```
{first_name} - shortest email you'll get all day. If I could help you
{how you help}, would that be useful?
```

### 4. Two sentences, straight to the point

Relevance plus a no-brainer ask. Works great as email 1 or email 3.

```
{first_name} - I saw {specific data point about their business, e.g. the 14
tools on your site}. Open to a free {audit / teardown} of what's actually
worth keeping?
```

### 5. Creative ideas (three ideas)

For offers that flex per customer (services, consulting, anything creative).

```
{first_name} - I was on your site and saw you help {their mission}. Had a few
ideas for you.

- {idea 1}
- {idea 2}
- {idea 3}

We help companies {how you help}. Worth a chat about running any of these?
```

### 6. Quick case study

Lead with a result close to their world.

```
{first_name} - {relevant first line}.

We just helped {case_study} hit {outcome}, and I think we could do the same
for you.

Want me to show you how?
```

### 7. Company founding story

When the "why we started" is the differentiator.

```
{first_name} - {relevant first line}.

We started {your company} because {one-line founding reason}. Since then we've
helped companies like {case_study} hit {result}.

If we could do the same for you, would that be useful?
```

### 8. Here goes nothing (casual, pattern interrupt)

One of the few times a subject line earns its keep.

```
Subject: here goes nothing

{first_name} - I know I'm just a rando in your inbox, so I'll keep it quick.

We're helping companies like {case_study} hit {outcome} in {timeframe}
without {no_good_thing}.

What proof could I send to earn a quick call?
```

### 9. PWJ classic (internal-discussion pattern interrupt)

```
{first_name} - any internal talks lately about {thing you help them do}? We're
helping companies like {company} hit {outcome} in {timeframe} without
{no_good_thing}. Is this a priority over there right now?
```

## Follow-up templates (email 2 and 3)

The follow-up should change the value prop, not repeat email 1. If email 1
talked about saving time, email 2 talks about making money. Lower the friction
on the ask each time.

### Three bullets of how we help (email 2)

Use the detail you cut from email 1.

```
{first_name} - to add some context, here's how we got {outcome}.

- {item 1}
- {item 2}
- {item 3}

Worth a quick chat?
```

### Analogy (email 2)

```
{first_name} - a good way to think about us: the same way you help people
{how they help}, we help you {how you help}.

{one line of proof}

No call needed, can I send a 2-minute video of how it works?
```

### Previous-company callout (email 2)

```
{first_name} - I'd guess you didn't leave {past_company} stoked to keep
worrying about {boring problem you solve}.

{messaging to test}

Open to seeing how we fix that?
```

### The "more I learn" line (email 2 filler when you have nothing better)

```
{first_name} - the more I learn about {company}, the more it looks like
{specific thing you'd actually research}. Is {problem} on your radar this
quarter?
```

### Breakup with a name handoff (email 3)

The strongest breakup. It signals the sequence is ending and reads personal
because it names a real teammate. Use the full name.

```
{first_name} - I know there are about {employee_count} people over at
{company}, so maybe {job to be done} isn't your call. Should I reach out to
{full_name_other_person} instead, given their role?
```

## Personalization opener bank

The opener answers "why are you reaching out to me." One sentence. Pick 3 to 5
that fit the ICP and rotate them. Which ones you can use depends on the data you
enrich in the 10-lead-list skill. Pass the test: if the same line could go to a
different prospect and still make sense, it is filler, not personalization.

### Scraped data, no AI needed

- Title and time in role: "Noticed you've been {title} for about {time} now, so
  figured you're the right person."
- Open job: "Saw you're hiring for a {title}."
- Open job plus keyword: "Saw you're hiring a {title}, and the post mentioned
  {keyword}."
- Tech on site: "Used BuiltWith and saw you're running {tech}."
- Reviews: "Saw the review {name} left about {summary of review}."
- Google rating: "Noticed the {rating}-star reviews on Google Maps."
- Web traffic: "SimilarWeb put you around {number} visitors a month."
- Google Ads: "Noticed you're bidding on {keyword}."
- Organic rank: "Searched {keyword} and saw you ranked {rank}."
- New role or promotion: "Noticed you recently took over as {title}."
- New leadership hire: "Saw {new_leader} just joined the team."
- Competitor compare: "{source} says people often compare you to {competitor}."
- Page mention: "Saw you mention {keyword} on this page: {link}."

### AI-generated from public data

- Company mission: "Saw how you help people {their mission} and wanted to reach
  out."
- Role plus mission: "Figured you're the right person, since {one-line reason
  from their profile}."
- LinkedIn post: "Saw your post about {summary of the post}."
- Recommendation: "Saw the recommendation {name} left about how you {summary}."
- Company news: "Saw the recent news about {summary}."
- How they'd use it: "Specifically, I think it could help you {how this company
  could use it, based on what they do}."

### Trigger-based (only fire when the signal is true)

- Recent funding (use as a filter, do not brag that you saw it).
- Headcount growth, hiring spree, new office.
- Product launch, partnership, award, conference talk.
- Stars aligning: only reach out when several of these are true at once. Highest
  reply rate, smallest pool. Move down the waterfall to your next-best signal
  when the pool runs thin.

## Subject line swipe

The subject is the first filter. Make it look like a note from a colleague or a
customer, without lying. 2 to 3 words. Lowercase often wins.

- billing ops
- attribution
- quick question
- pizza orders (hit 88% open rate for a restaurant campaign)
- outbound edge
- saw your post
- competitor insights
- show and tell
- here goes nothing

Watch the preview text too. The first line of the email shows up next to the
subject, so lead with the researched opener, not boilerplate. A great subject
plus a generic first line still reads as spam.

Avoid: ALL CAPS, exclamation marks, money symbols, and "free," "guaranteed,"
"limited time."

## CTA swipe

Make the ask worth their time, and make it easy to say yes. Soft yes/no asks
lower friction. A direct ask works when the offer and niche are strong.

Soft yes/no asks:

- Worth a look?
- Worth exploring?
- Is this of interest?
- Open to learning more?
- Worth a conversation?
- Feel free to say no if it's not a fit, but is this worth a look?

Lower-friction-than-a-call asks (good for email 2 and 3):

- No need for a call. Can I send a 2-minute video of how it works?
- If I made a short Loom walking through it for you, would you watch it?
- What proof could I send to earn a quick call?

Make-it-worth-their-time asks (tie the ask to the payoff):

- If we could {specific outcome} with about {small effort}, would you take me up
  on it?
- If I could help you {outcome} without {no_good_thing}, is that worth 15
  minutes?

End on a high note. The last line decides a lot of replies. "Thank you" beats
"Best." A genuine compliment ("either way, great work on the podcast") lifts
reply rates.

## One worked example (all the moves at once)

This is the Josh Braun CFO email, kept as an illustration of how the pieces
stack: a real trigger, an expensive problem, a poke-the-bear question, specific
proof, a soft CTA, and a warm closer. Do not copy it. Build the user's own from
their inputs.

```
Subject: eastman building

Lisa - noticed your Eastman building isn't at full capacity with most of the
team remote.

Curious, what are you doing to avoid overpaying property taxes on it?

Asking because Beth Smith, CFO at {case_study} (235,000 sq ft), used a
lesser-known approach to cut property taxes 5.2%. It comes down to external
obsolescence.

Open to how she did it?

Either way, smart move picking a spot next to Karen's Cafe. Best walnut
chocolate chip cookies around.
```

Why it works: the opener proves real homework, the question pokes a cost they
may be ignoring (loss aversion), the proof is specific and named, the ask is a
low-friction yes/no, and the close makes them smile.
